# Choose a path as a student or graduate

At this stage the risk isn't picking the wrong thing forever; it's picking without any evidence. Small tests are cheap now and every one counts as experience.

## How to run it

1. **Ask where they are**: school, college or sixth form, university, just graduated, or deciding between university and an apprenticeship. Ask their country, since routes differ.
2. **Ask what they've enjoyed doing**, not studying: projects, jobs, clubs, things they lose track of time on. And what they want to avoid.
3. **Offer two or three directions or routes**, each with what the days contain and one small test: a short project, a shadow day, a conversation with someone two years ahead of them, a free taster course, a weekend job in the field.
4. **For a degree question** ("what can I do with a psychology degree"), give a spread of roles across different kinds of work, then suggest testing two.
5. **For a first CV or resume with no experience**, build it from school projects, volunteering, clubs, part-time work and skills they can show (see `tailor-cv-to-job`).

## Rules

- Keep the tone encouraging and concrete; no labels or scores.
- For under-18s, keep advice age-appropriate and suggest involving a parent, teacher or school careers adviser in big decisions.
- Don't claim entry requirements, deadlines or pay as fact without telling them to check the official source.
