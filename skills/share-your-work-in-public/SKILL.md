# Sharing your work in public

For a career changer, a post about a small piece of real work in the new field says more than "open to new opportunities". People hiring in a field notice who is already doing the work, and each post becomes portfolio material.

## Start from what they actually did

Ask what they tried, what happened and what surprised them. Good material:

- a 15-minute test of the work and the one thing it showed
- a practice project, with the decision they found hardest
- what someone in the job told them (with permission)
- a first attempt and what they changed

If they haven't done anything yet, suggest a small test first (see `test-a-career`) rather than a post about intentions.

## Shape of the post

1. What they tried, in one or two plain sentences, with one specific detail: a number, a name, a moment.
2. What they noticed.
3. One question to people who do this work. Replies are where introductions start.

40 to 120 words on LinkedIn; under 280 characters on X (a link counts as 23). Label practice work as practice. Never imply clients or results they didn't have.

## Other moves

- **Headline**: where they're heading and what they bring, never a title they haven't held. Recruiter search on LinkedIn matches on meaning now, so plain words work.
- **Comment**: two to four sentences under a post by someone in the field, adding something only they could say. No praise-only comments and no self-promotion. On X, replies inside a niche are the main way new accounts get seen.
- **Message**: three sentences to one person: why them, one specific question, an easy way to say no.

## Cadence

Two or three posts a week at most, plus a thoughtful comment most days. Staying on one field helps both feeds find the right readers. After three or four posts, help them turn the series into a short case study.

## Voice check before they post

Flag, and suggest a fix for: long dashes, emoji bullets, more than three hashtags, stock openers ("excited to share", "here's what I learned"), words like journey, leverage, game-changer, and a post with nothing only they could know. Readers and LinkedIn's "Seems like AI slop" flag both punish these.

## Rules

- Their words first. Edit, don't ghostwrite.
- Never add facts, numbers, employers or titles.
- Never post for them.
- Under 18: keep it to sharing with people they know; don't push public posting.
