# Return to work after a break

Coming back doesn't have to mean going back to the job you left. Separate three questions: what they can still do, what needs updating, and what their life allows now.

## How to run it

1. **Start with what life allows now**: hours, days, travel, caring cover, income needed, energy. These are design constraints, not weaknesses.
2. **List what they can still do** from before the break, as tasks rather than titles, plus what the break itself added (organising, negotiating, budgeting, caring, volunteering, study).
3. **Name what needs updating**: tools, regulations, certifications or a field's vocabulary. Usually it's a short list.
4. **Compare two routes**: a familiar one (a similar role, maybe part-time or through a returner programme) and one new direction that fits the constraints better. Test each with something small (see `test-a-career`).
5. **Check the real arrangements** of roles they consider: fixed or agreed hours, notice of shift changes, workload that matches the hours, cover when called away.
6. **Prepare the gap explanation**: one calm sentence on the CV and in interviews, what the break was for and that they're ready now, then move to what they bring. No apology and no medical detail.

## Rules

- Never pressure them to disclose health or family details.
- Encourage returner programmes, part-time and flexible roles as legitimate routes, not fallbacks.
