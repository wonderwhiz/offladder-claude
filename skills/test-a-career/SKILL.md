# Test a career before committing

The idea behind OffLadder: you learn whether you fit a kind of work by doing a small, real piece of it, not by describing yourself or reading about it. A test should take an evening to a weekend, fit around a current job, and produce something another person can react to.

## How to run it

1. **Name the direction in their words.** If they have two or three in mind, test the one they are most curious about first and plan a second for comparison.
2. **Find the smallest real unit of the work.** Every job has one: one chart for an analyst, one redrawn screen for a UX designer, one lesson for a teacher, one afternoon on site for a trade. Ask what people in that work actually do on a normal Tuesday.
3. **Shrink it to one task they can finish this week**, with a time box (two to three hours is usually enough) and a clear output.
4. **Add a reaction from a practitioner.** Suggest they show the result to one person who does the work and ask what they would fix. That reaction is evidence their own opinion can't give.
5. **Decide what to notice.** Before they start, ask them to write down which part they expect to enjoy. Afterwards: which twenty minutes would they repeat tomorrow? What did they put off?
6. **Plan the second run.** Novelty makes almost anything feel good once. The same kind of task twice, or a harder version, is the real test.

## If the OffLadder tools are connected

- `find_career_experiment` with the career they named returns a written experiment from OffLadder's library, and a link to carry on with OffLadder's coach.

- `find_career_guide` with "test a career before switching" returns the full method with sources.

## Rules

- Never present a direction as a match or a verdict. It is a hypothesis to test.
- For regulated work they can't practise yet (medicine, law, nursing, piloting), test the safe parts: shadowing, conversations with practitioners, and a taste of the study it requires.
- Don't suggest quitting, paying for a course or retraining before a direction has survived two or three tests.
- Keep the output to one experiment they can start this week, written as numbered steps.
