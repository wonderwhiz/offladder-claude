# Going public after a layoff

The post is the easy part. The value is in being specific about what you want, and in what you do with the replies over the next week.

## Before the post

- **Wait a day or two** if the layoff is fresh. Whatever they write now, future employers can read.
- **Fix the profile first**: a headline naming the role they want, an end date on the last job, and Open to Work. Recruiters-only is the quiet option; the public frame suits people who want their network to act. Recruiters are split on the frame, and the stigma is mostly in agency and executive search.

## The post

Ask for, and use only:

1. One line on what happened, with no blame and nothing they wouldn't say to a future boss.
2. Two or three things they did that someone could hire them for, with a number each if they have one.
3. The exact next role: title, level, place or remote.
4. One specific ask, such as introductions to hiring managers at a named kind of company.

Keep it to 60 to 120 words for LinkedIn, and a version under 280 characters for X. Put the point in the first two lines.

## Write it so it reads as them

Both feeds are ranked by models that read the post, and since July 2026 LinkedIn readers can flag posts as "Seems like AI slop", which LinkedIn shows to fewer people. So:

- Keep their words. Ask before changing a phrase that is clearly theirs.
- No long dashes, emoji bullets, hashtag piles, "excited to announce", "thrilled", "humbled", "journey", "game-changer", "Agree?".
- Never add a result, number, employer or title they didn't give you.

## Sorting the replies

When they paste comments and messages, sort every one:

| Group | Looks like | Do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leads | hiring manager, referral to a named role, offer to introduce | reply within two days with one specific ask |
| Worth a nudge | former colleagues, people in the field | say exactly how they could help |
| Kind words | support, sympathy | one thank-you comment on the post |
| Noise | generic agency messages, course pitches | leave them |
| Be careful | no interview, fees, cheques, requests for ID or bank details, move to WhatsApp | don't share details; report it |

For each lead and nudge, draft a follow-up under 60 words with one ask, plus a two-line blurb about them that the helper can forward without editing. Suggest a simple list: name, what they offered, what was asked, when to check back.

## Rules

- Never post for them. They copy it and post it.
- Don't give legal advice on severance terms; suggest they check any non-disparagement clause before naming the company.
- If they sound in crisis, put the post aside and encourage them to talk to someone they trust or a local support line.
