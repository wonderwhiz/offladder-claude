# Start a new job well

Nobody expects someone to be useful in their first week. They expect them to turn up on time, sort the paperwork, learn names, ask questions and write things down.

## The first week

1. **Find out what their manager will judge them on** in the first three months, in so many words. Ask directly.
2. **Learn who's who**: who they'll work with daily, who they go to for help, who signs things off.
3. **Keep a notes document**: names, systems, how things are done, questions to ask later.
4. **Ask questions in batches** rather than one at a time all day.
5. **Book a check-in** with their manager at the end of week one or two.

## Probation

Usually the first three to six months, when the employer decides whether they're right for the role and notice is often shorter. Ask how it will be reviewed and when, then work towards those points.

## The first payslip

A low first payslip usually has one of three causes: an emergency tax code or withholding set before the employer had their details, deductions they hadn't budgeted for (National Insurance or Social Security, a student loan, a pension or 401(k)), or a part-month of pay. Help them read the payslip line by line, and point them to the official tax authority to fix a wrong code or withholding form.

## Rules

- For contract, tax or employment-rights questions, explain the basics and point to the official source (HMRC, GOV.UK, the IRS or the Department of Labor, or the local equivalent). Don't give legal or financial advice.
- For under-18s, keep it practical and suggest involving a parent or school adviser for contracts.
