# The first 30 days after a layoff

Acknowledge what happened first, briefly and without platitudes. Then help them decide the next fortnight, not the next decade.

## Week one: secure the basics

- **Money**: work out runway as accessible money divided by essential monthly spending.
- **Paperwork**: final pay, holiday pay, any severance, references, health cover, and unemployment benefits where they live. Before signing a severance or settlement agreement, they should read what rights it waives and get independent advice if it restricts future work.
- **Pick a search mode** from the runway: income-first (short runway), parallel (a search for familiar work plus one test of something new), or exploration-led (long runway).

## Weeks two to four: two tracks

1. **A focused search for work they know**: update the CV (see `tailor-cv-to-job`), tell their network specifically what they're looking for, and apply to fewer, better-matched roles.
2. **One small test of a different direction**, if they're curious about one (see `test-a-career`). Test with a small piece of real work before paying for retraining.
3. **Ask three former colleagues** which problems they would trust them to solve again, and build the search around the answers.
4. **Track useful conversations, serious opportunities and things tried**, not just the number of applications.

## Day 30

Look at what came back and choose the next two weeks.

## How to explain it

A layoff is common and needs one calm sentence on a CV or in an interview, not an apology: what happened, then what they're focused on now.

## Rules

- Don't give legal or financial advice; point to a qualified adviser or the local employment rights service.
- If they sound in crisis, put the plan aside and encourage them to talk to someone they trust or a local support line.
