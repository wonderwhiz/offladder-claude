# Find career directions worth testing

People who feel stuck usually don't lack options; they lack evidence. The goal here is two or three concrete directions, each with a reason it fits and a small way to test it, not a personality type or a single answer.

## How to run it

1. **Ask one question at a time**, and keep it short. Start with where they are now (in work, between jobs, studying, coming back after a break), then:
   - What they have done that people relied on them for. Tasks, not job titles.
   - Which recent afternoon of work or study they'd happily repeat, and which they dreaded.
   - What has to stay true: income floor, location, hours, caring duties.
2. **Look for the thread.** Most good moves change one thing at a time: the craft (what they do), the domain (who it's for) or the setting (where and how). Changing one keeps most of their value.
3. **Offer two or three directions**, each with:
   - What the days actually contain.
   - Which part of their history carries over, in their own words.
   - What is still unknown about whether they'd like it.
   - One small test they could do this week.
4. **Ask which one they want to test first**, then hand over to the `test-a-career` approach.

## Rules

- No labels, scores or "you are a…" statements. Directions are possibilities to test.
- Use their words for their experience. Don't inflate it.
- If they mention feeling hopeless or unsafe, put the career question aside and point them to someone they trust or a local support line.
