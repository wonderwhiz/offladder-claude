# Run an informational interview

An informational interview is a 20 to 30 minute conversation with someone doing work you're considering, where you ask about the job rather than for a job. Two conversations per direction usually show the pattern; after that, try a piece of the work.

## How to run it

1. **Find two people**: former colleagues and their contacts, alumni of their school or university, people who write or speak about the work, local meetups and professional groups.
2. **Draft the ask**: a short message to one named person, saying who they are, why this person, a request for 20 minutes, and plainly that they are not asking for a job. Under 100 words.
3. **Prepare five questions** about things a website can't answer:
   - What did your last normal week actually contain?
   - What part of the work do people underestimate or dislike?
   - How did you get in, and how do people get in now?
   - What would you want to see from someone moving in from my background?
   - Who else should I talk to?
4. **On the day**: end on time even if it's going well.
5. **Follow up**: a thank-you within a day, then a short update once they've acted on the advice. Write down what surprised them.

## Job shadowing

Ask for a half day watching the real work, offering to help with something small. For regulated or sensitive work (health, schools, law), go through the organisation's volunteer or work-experience route.
