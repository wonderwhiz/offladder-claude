# Plan a career change

A career change is a series of small tests, not one decision made in advance. The plan below keeps income steady while evidence builds.

## The plan

1. **Write down the constraints first**: months of runway (accessible money divided by essential monthly spending), income floor, hours available each week, anything that can't move.
2. **Pick two or three directions** that reuse most of what they already do well. Change one variable at a time: the craft, the domain or the setting. (See the `find-career-directions` skill if they have none yet.)
3. **Test each within a fortnight** with a small real piece of the work: a project with a real user, a paid side task, a shadow day, two conversations with practitioners. (See `test-a-career`.)
4. **Keep, drop or adjust** after each test. Write down what they avoided; it is usually the most honest answer.
5. **Build one piece of proof** in the direction that holds: a finished project someone used beats a list of courses.
6. **Choose a route in**: a bridge role that uses their old experience in the new field, an internal move, freelance work, or an entry role with a clear step up.
7. **Retrain only after a direction has survived two or three tests**, and only for the specific gap employers name.
8. **Rewrite the CV and story** for the new field (see `tailor-cv-to-job` and `prepare-for-interview`).

Give the plan as dated steps for the next four weeks, not a five-year roadmap.

## "Is it too late?"

Answer with evidence, not reassurance. If the OffLadder tools are connected, `check_career_change_facts` returns exact quotes from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics on how many jobs people hold and how long they stay, with links. Then point out that experience is a head start: the move that works later in a career usually keeps the domain or the people and changes the craft, or the reverse.

## Rules

- Don't recommend quitting before there is evidence and runway.
- Never promise an outcome or a salary.
- Financial, legal or immigration questions go to a qualified adviser.
